# Review round sixteen: one review of `43ca919`, in depth, triaged

The reviewer went past the diff this time, with three readers: one trying to break round fifteen's fixes, one on the server, the service worker and the privacy promise, one on the offline corpus build, which no round had read closely. Sixteen findings and two design notes, every one checked against the source here and real, all sixteen done; the two design notes are both taken. Three findings are marked by the reviewer as not present in today's corpus (6, 7, 8) and are fixed in the build so the next refresh cannot produce them. Item 3 is the one already on the live site, and it has a corpus step for the deployer. In the reviewer's order.

## Lost or wrong data

1. **"Stop syncing" in one tab could still be undone by another tab's run** (1; a fault in round fifteen's fix, which checked only the tab's own memory). Tab A wrote `null` and cleared the outbox, and only then broadcast; a run under way in tab B passed its check and wrote the old meta back, key included, so the device was syncing again after a reload with an emptied outbox, and with "Replace from backup" tab B could fold a batch of the old vault into the freshly replaced log and push the backup into the vault the grower had just left. Fixed in `src/lib/db/vault.ts` and `src/lib/sync/engine.svelte.ts`: `save()` reads and writes the meta in one IndexedDB transaction and refuses unless the stored record still carries the run's key; the same check (`live()`) runs before a batch is folded and before an outbox ack. A run that fails it drops the vault in memory and ends. Engine test: the stored meta set to null mid-run by "another tab", the run ends with the stop error, nothing is written back, the batch in flight is not folded. Vault test on a real in-memory IndexedDB (`fake-indexeddb`, new dev dependency): the conditional write refuses once the record is null or another vault's.
2. **Stopping and then creating or joining a vault while a push was in flight emptied the new vault's outbox** (2). The old run's ack removed entries `setup()` had just refilled for the new vault, its error landed on the new vault's status, and its `finally` cleared `busy` in the middle of the new run. Every run now belongs to a generation, bumped by `forget()`, by `setup()` and by a drop; a stale run acks nothing (the check before the ack is item 1's), sets no error, clears no busy flag and counts no run. Engine test: a push held at the POST while the grower stops and creates a second vault; the second vault's status is clean and a third device joining it receives the plant.
3. **1.7% of published photographs were credited to "unknown" under CC BY** (3; the reviewer's sample: 146 of 8,741 photographs across 298 species; on the corpus here, 2,935 photographs in 702 dossiers). The build's credit fell back to "unknown", Commons' to "Wikimedia Commons", iNaturalist's to "iNaturalist user"; CC BY and CC BY-SA require the author's name, and "photographs carry author and licence" is one of the site's rules. Fixed in the build (`photosFromMedia`, `sources/wikimedia.ts`, `sources/inat.ts`): a photograph with no author is not published unless it is CC0, which asks for none. For what is already on disk, `npm run dossier -- --prune-uncredited` removes them from every dossier with no upstream call (run here on the corpus: 2,935 removed from 702 dossiers; 53 species are left without a photograph until a fill gives them a credited one), then `--index` and the upload; `docs/DEPLOY.md` has the four lines.
4. **Imported v2 events without an id still collided across devices, and round fifteen's guard then kept two devices apart for good** (4). An event without an id was named by a count across the whole file, so a device that skipped an earlier plant gave the same event a different id under the same stamp; the vault kept whichever arrived first and the screen applied both until a reload, and the ledger took the skipped change's number too. Three fixes: the fallback id is the event's place within its own plant (`v2-<acc>-e<n>`), the same on every device; `storeIn` keeps, of two changes under one stamp, the one that ranks higher by content, whichever arrived first, so every device converges, and returns what it kept, which is all `commit()` applies and all that goes on the ledger. Import test: the same event gets the same id and stamp whether or not the plant before it was skipped. Vault test: two pairs under one stamp, met in either order, leave the same log; the declined change's number is not on the ledger.
5. **Plants already revived by rounds ten to fourteen stayed revived** (5). Round fifteen's guard prevented new revivals and repaired nothing. On load, a record whose latest removal is followed only by an `importedOn` stamp (what those builds wrote on every imported record, tombstones included) is removed again, once, as an ordinary change that syncs; a record edited in any other way after its removal is a real revival and is left alone. Store test: the round-fourteen shape is gone after a reload, an intentional revival stays, and a third load writes nothing more.
6. **The corpus build published "in range" counts when the range was never checked** (6; not in today's corpus). With no boxes (Kew's distribution refused, or a country-level range) every record counted as in range. The dossier now carries `rangeTested: false` in that case, and the species page says the range source did not answer and no record was tested, or, for a country-level range, that the records were not tested against it, instead of "inside the native range". Build test with the distribution call refused.
7. **A NASA POWER refusal became "no daily extremes are on file", changed the cold floor, and was never retried** (7; not in today's corpus). The refusal was recorded only as text, `upstream.climate` stayed `ok`, so `needsRebuild` never asked again; the floor fell back to the CHELSA coldest mean night and the page said none were on file. The climate now carries `extremesStatus` (ok, none, refused, skipped), the build marks it as `upstream['climate.extremes']` so a refusal is rebuilt like any other, the sheet buckets carry it, and the cold-floor sentence, the glance and compare say "the daily extremes were not checked (NASA POWER did not answer when this page was built)" where they said "no daily extremes are on file". Climate test and sheet test.
8. **Two species with one name gave the plain slug to whichever file sorted first** (8; not in today's corpus, 0 suffixed slugs among 8,947). The plain slug now goes to the accepted name and the other gets its key appended, with the lower key keeping it between two of one status, so the outcome does not depend on file order; each sheet is filed under, and carries, the index slug, so a suffixed homonym's sheet cannot answer to the plain name.

## What leaves the device

9. **No referrer policy, so private URLs went to the server with every request** (9; the reviewer's one thing to fix). The browser's default sends the full same-origin URL as `Referer`, so every sheets, entries, names and forecast request, and SvelteKit's version poll, carried the page it came from: `/plants/2026-0015`, `/plants/new?species=…`, the front page's search in `?q=`. `<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer">` in `app.html`, and a `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer` header on every response: from a new `src/hooks.server.ts` for what the Worker renders, and from `_headers` (project root, as adapter-cloudflare wants it) for the prerendered pages and the build's files. Checked on the wire here with a logging proxy in front of the server: no `Referer` on any font, API or data request. e2e: the meta on the document, both headers on a dynamic page, a prerendered page, the offline page and an API route. `/about/how` says it.
10. **After a deploy, the service worker sent query-string navigations on private pages to the server, and cached each one** (10). The shell match included the query, so `/labels?acc=…`, `/labels?loc=…` and `/plants?show=…` went to the network whenever the post-deploy reload made a navigation a full load, and each was cached per URL. The shell match ignores the query, and a fetched shell is cached under its path alone.
11. **The privacy list left out what else was sent** (11). A `cultifolio.hemi` cookie derived from the site's coordinates went with every request, sync included; the frost watch sent the place's altitude and full-precision coordinates, rounded only on the server. The cookie is now scoped to `/species` and `/compare`, the two paths that read it, so it rides on nothing else (an old `path=/` copy is expired); coordinates are rounded to a hundredth of a degree and altitude to ten metres on the device before the request is made; `/about/how`, the README and the Show HN draft list the coordinates with the altitude, both cookies (units, and the one-letter hemisphere with where it goes) and the referrer. e2e: the cookie is on the species and compare pages' jar and not on the API's or sync's.

## Blocked, stale or wasteful

12. **After a corpus refresh, a device could keep the old reference until the next deploy** (12). `/api/entries` never compared `?c=` with the corpus it held and always answered `max-age=86400`; the sheets route answered `no-store` on a mismatch but the service worker stored any `ok` answer cache-first anyway. An isolate still holding the old index answered under the new id and the worker kept it. The entries route now makes the same check the sheets route does, and the worker does not store a `no-store` answer. Route test for entries; the worker change is by reading.
13. **With the vault full and the outbox empty, every run uploaded a whole photograph to be told it was still full** (13; round fifteen's probe, which was mine). On every focus and every five minutes while visible, on mobile data. The probe with a photograph is at most hourly; the batch push still probes with a small request whenever the outbox has something.

## Confusing

14. **A multi-plant add that failed partway left the form ready to add them all again** (14). Plants 1 and 2 of 5 existed, the form said "Add 5 plants", and pressing it added five more; the notice said "Free space" when the refusal was a number already used. The count drops to what remains and the notice says how many were added and numbered; a number clash is worded as one, without the free-space line.
15. **The about pages could be framed by any site** (15). The prerendered `/about/how`, `/about/formats` and `/offline` carried no `frame-ancestors`, the CSP being a meta tag there, which cannot carry it. `_headers` gives them `X-Frame-Options: DENY`, and the hook gives the dynamic pages the same beside the CSP. Covered by item 9's e2e.
16. **`POST /api/sync/vault` with a JSON `null` was a 500** (16). A body that is valid JSON but not an object is treated as no body: 400. Engine test over `null`, a number, a string and an empty body.
