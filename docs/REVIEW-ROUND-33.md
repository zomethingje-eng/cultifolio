# Review round thirty-three: three reviews of `f349edb` (and one of `5260d76`), triaged

The first reviewer's round thirty (fourteen findings on `f349edb`, in depth, from the salvage rule to the synonym redirect), the second reviewer's round twenty-two (seven findings on `f349edb`, with fresh Lighthouse runs) and the second reviewer's round twenty-one, which reviewed `5260d76` and whose three findings (a half-made plant from one bad required field, the photo pixels left behind by a failed record write, the release suite's UTC "yesterday") round thirty fixed before it arrived; its two standing points are noted at the end. Every finding was checked in the source or reproduced. "R1-n" is the first reviewer's; "R2-n" the second reviewer's round twenty-two; the round-twenty-one review is "R2a". Three of the four reviewers' first choices were the same defect, and it is fixed first.

## The salvage rule, in the fold

1. **A set-aside batch could still leave a half-made plant that threw on the plants page** (R1-1, R2-1, R2a-1; reproduced by two reviewers with two devices). Round thirty's rule dropped every change to a record whose required field could not be read, but only within one list: a batch set aside whole had created the plant, a later batch edited its price, and the fold made a live plant with a price and no name. The rule is in the fold now, in one place: a record without the fields its kind cannot be shown without (a plant's name; a batch's name, method and date; a place's name; an event's plant, day and type; a photograph's day; a species record's name) is in the fold but not live, and is counted; the sync page says "N records wait for changes this build cannot read yet". No path can make a half record show: a pull, a restore, an import, or any order of them. Two things followed. The door no longer drops every change to a record because one could not be read (R2-1's second case: a bad first name and a good later one lost the plant; the fold keeps the good one now, whichever list it came in), and a required field takes no `null` (R2-1's first case: `taxonName: null` passed the type check and folded to a plant with no name). And a number written as text ("3" in a count) is mended to the number as a number in a text field already was, so an old batch is not set aside on every device for good (R1-4). Unit tests for each case, including the one the reviewers reproduced.
2. **The mend rewrote words this build does not know** (R1-2, R2-2). "tissue culture" became "seed" and "f2" became "unknown" wherever the log was read, including before a push, so a newer build's method could be turned into a different one by a pass through an older device, and the propagation pages' "shown by its word" could never run. Only the v2 importer's exact spellings are mended now (its method labels lower-cased with the "s" cut, and the provenance texts it passed through: "Wild collected", "ex habitat" and a few more); every other word folds as it is. The guess at a provenance from free text is gone with it (R1-5: "not wild collected" became `wild`), since a wrong claim about wild origin is worse than the word itself. The edit forms carry a value they do not know as an extra option, so a plant of `nameKind: grex` edited for its price keeps `grex`, and a batch under a method this build does not know keeps its provenance (R1-2's two form cases, both reproduced). Unit tests for the old labels and for unknown ones.
3. **The app refused its own backup once the log passed about 74 MB** (R1-3, R2-3; both reproduced). Round thirty's total-size cap bounded the declared sizes by the file's size, and `changes.json` deflates by an order of magnitude. The deflated entries (the log, the manifest, the sheets) have their own caps and are out of the sum; the sum is of the stored entries, the photographs, which is what the cap was for. Unit test: a 70 MB note round-trips through `buildBackup` and `readBackup` in a file under 2 MB, and the many-entry bomb is still refused.

## The reference's rules

4. **The typical cell could jump to an outlier** (R1-7, reproduced with a synthetic grid). The land search took the first mostly-land candidate among the twelve nearest the median coldest night, however far its own night was from it, so a fog-belt coast could hand its cold floor to a mountain cell. Candidates are tried only while their coldest night is within 2 °C of the median; when none is mostly land the nearest stands (it had kept the most-land candidate, against its own comment) and the source line says how many were tried. Unit test with the reviewer's probe.
5. **The hemisphere split fired on equatorial ranges** (R1-8, reproduced). Five cells at 2–6° N and five at 1–5° S split, half the range went, and the page said the other side's seasons were six months apart, which is false at 5°. Only cells poleward of 10° count as being on a side, which is the line `/about/how` already draws for a season to reverse; when the smaller side has three such cells and a fifth of them, its poleward cells are left out and the cells within 10° of the equator stay in either way. The naturalised case the reviewer raises (Mexico plus three naturalised South African cells) is a range question, not a hemisphere one, and is noted below. Unit test: the equatorial range is one climate of ten cells.
6. **The records count overstated what the envelope rests on** (R1-8, R2-4; live on Rhipsalis baccifera: 407 cells "holding the 862 in-range records" while 155 cells were left out). The count is of the records in the cells the envelope reads, per cell, and the source line says "holding N of the M in-range records". The page's own sentence was reworded in round thirty-two for the sea cells and now agrees.
7. **The no-accuracy rule was broken by rounding** (R1-9, reproduced). The decimals were counted after `toFixed(3)`, which drops a trailing zero, so −33.120 counted as two and about one precise phone record in five failed on that alone. Counted on the coordinate as the record gives it. Unit test with −25.12046. The reviewer also asks that herbarium records with four or five decimals and no stated uncertainty be admitted; not taken, with the reason below.
8. **Wording that overclaimed** (R1-11). The sea-cell notice said no in-range cell was mostly land after trying twelve; it says which were tried. The evidence paragraph called records with no stated accuracy "placed to worse than 10 km"; it says what they are. `/about/how` said such records were admitted; it says the rule, and describes the land layer, the hemisphere rule and the typical-cell search, none of which it had. "Asked 2026-09-29" was printed on rows an offline rederive had carried from an earlier build; the date is shown only for a source the build asked.
9. **"Grouped as a other epiphyte"** (R2-7, live). The article follows the label now (an other epiphyte, a cactus or succulent, an orchid), in the cultivation section, the short note, the cold-floor card and the compare page.

## The synonym redirect and the search

10. **The redirect** (R1-12, R2-6). It was a 301, which a browser keeps for good while the backbone changes its mind; it is a 302 cached for the day the answer is. A fuzzy match was accepted at any confidence; it needs 90 or better and the same genus, or an exact match. A variety's accepted usage is the variety, so `…-var-radula` said "not in the reference" when its species was; the species key is tried too. A hybrid slug's `x-` is not a word. A lookup upstream is rate-limited per address (sixty in ten minutes), since a script can mint binomial-shaped addresses without end. And `?was=` printed whatever was written into the link as a statement ("Cannabis sativa is a synonym…"); it is shown only when the name is one the species' own record lists. Unit tests.
11. **Synonym cuts and matching** (R1-13). A section, a subgenus, an undetermined name or a misapplied one (auct.) is not a name of a species and is no longer cut into one ("Opuntia sect" is gone). A query's words that fall to older names must come from one older name: "aloe margaritifera" found Tulista pumila through two of its synonyms, which is a name nobody wrote; and a rank marker (var, subsp) is not a search word. The OpenAlex title test for a specimen record dropped a paper "…inferred from herbarium specimens"; it tests a specimen's title shape and DiSSCo's venue, not the words. Unit tests. Homonyms merged by dropping authorship, and the cap of six other-genus-first, stand: the index carries binomials for the search and the cap keeps the index small; a label's same-genus old name is on the species page in full.

## Not taken, with reasons

**Below-sea-level land as sea** (R1-10). The rule already is the one the reviewer asks for: a cell is sea only when no ETOPO pixel in it is at or above sea level (the packer masks the sea pixels and averages the rest). What reads as sea is a cell whose every pixel is below zero: the Dead Sea shore, Death Valley's floor, Qattara, Turfan, the Caspian lowland. A named list of depressions is a hand-written exception in a derived pipeline, and the honest fix is a land mask in the packer (Natural Earth's polygons, which the maps already use), which is a packer change and a grid rebuild for after the post. The 1,483 species with a cell at sea are mostly coasts: a record placed to 10 km on a shore lands in a 5 km cell offshore often enough, and the two spot checks (Copiapoa cinerea one cell of 53, Yucca glauca none) are what that looks like. **Herbarium records to four decimals** (R1-9): a sheet georeferenced after the fact carries no evidence of precision in its decimals (a gazetteer gives five), and the uncertainty field is where that belongs; the rule stays at a person's observation. **The pot guard across devices** (R1, small): a form check, as before; a clamp in the batch figures goes with the log editor. **The naturalised-cells case** (R1-8): needs a range test on the records, which is the map marker's next step. **Exact accession search before Today** (R2a, R2, standing): still the second reviewer's design preference and still noted.
