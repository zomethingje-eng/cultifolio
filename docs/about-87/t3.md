## /about/how

### 1. Five new parts at the end of "Your collection" (h2 `#collection`), before "Browse, with your collection"

#### A plant's growing season (h3 `#season`)
Summary: A plant can have a growing season: a winter-grower, a summer-grower, or no rest, with the months it rests. In those months Today does not count it as due, and says it is resting and why.
- A plant's page suggests a season from its species page's reading of the habitat (what the rules read (#year)), says it is a suggestion, and names the rule that made it. Nothing is written until you confirm it or set your own.
- The rain rule's winter or summer growing season suggests that season, resting in every month outside the rainy months, shifted to your hemisphere as the season card shifts them.
- Under 120 mm of rain a year, the temperature rule's cooler six months suggest a winter-grower resting in the other six. Rain spread over eight months or more suggests no rest.
- A rainy season at the year's mean temperature, a flat temperature curve, a wet season with nights below freezing, or no season at all suggests nothing; nor does a hybrid, which has no one habitat.
- A suggestion reads the habitat's rain and temperature, not the plant. You can change its months, and set a season on any plant.
- In a plant's rest months Today lists it under its space as resting, with no Water button, in one line that says the season, the months, and whether you confirmed it from the habitat or set it yourself.
- A plant's rest months add to its space's dry months: a plant with no rest, in a space kept dry in winter, is kept dry there.
- A plant with a season set is not listed apart in its habitat's dry season; its own months decide. The watering calendar leaves its rest months out too.
#### Papers kept with a plant (h3 `#attachments`)
Summary: A plant's page keeps its papers: a CITES permit or certificate, a phytosanitary certificate, an invoice, as a PDF, JPEG or PNG of up to 10 MB. They are kept, synced and backed up as its photographs are.
- A file is taken by its own first bytes, never by its name: a PDF, a JPEG or a PNG, up to 10 MB, and thirty a plant.
- It is kept in this browser as a photograph is, not encrypted on the device. With sync on it goes into the vault sealed on the device, and the device that receives it checks its hash.
- Open shows it in a new tab from the file on this device; Download saves it under its own name. A paper whose file has not yet come from the device that added it says so.
- A backup holds each paper's file and a restore puts it back. A version of the app made before papers keeps one as a record waiting for fields, syncs and backs up its file, and never shows it as a photograph.
#### Labels (h3 `#labels`)
Summary: Labels print on Avery sheets, on strips to cut, or on label tape 12, 18 or 24 mm wide for a P-touch-style printer. Each label's QR code carries the plant's number.
- The code is the plant's address by its number, such as /plants/2026-0012. A sheet label's code adds the species and the name after a #, which stays on the phone.
- The plant's page finds the plant by that number in the collection on this device, so it opens with no network, as the rest of your collection does. Any phone's camera shows the number in the address it reads.
- A code printed before carries the plant's identity, and still opens it. A label whose plant was renumbered since opens a page that gives the plant's new number.
- Another collection may give the same number. When a scanned label's name is not your plant's, its page says so and offers the label's species, as it does for a label from a plant you do not have.
- Label tape prints one label a page, 50, 60 or 70 mm long, the code and the text 1.5, 2 or 3 mm clear of each edge of the tape. Choose the tape printer in the print dialog, set its tape, and print at 100%.
- A backup label is a second label for each plant with the number alone, as large as the label holds, for the back or the bottom of the pot.
- The large number prints the number at 14 points on a sheet label (9 on the pot-rim stock), or over a third of the tape's width, with the name on one line.
- A label's code and its care line have their room from the start, so nothing on the sheet moves as they arrive.
#### The plants sheet and the report (h3 `#exports`)
Summary: My plants downloads the plants as a spreadsheet, one column a field, and prints a collection report with photographs, for an insurer or a society show.
- The sheet is the backup's plants.csv. After the columns it has always had come the GBIF key, the plant's own watering rhythm, the page its name is an older name of, its season, rest months and where the season came from, its number of photographs and its papers, then its id.
- The log's bookkeeping (a notes edit's base, an import's key, the cover's id) is left out; the whole log is in the backup. An import of the sheet reads back the columns it always has, and names the others apart.
- The report lists each growing plant, or every plant, by number, with its cover photograph, name, provenance, source, space, season and papers, and its price and notes if you choose. It is made on this device and printed, or saved as a PDF, from the print dialog.
- Prices are totalled by currency as written, never converted.
#### The app lock (h3 `#lock`)
Summary: Settings can set a passcode this device asks for before it shows the app. It is a screen over the app, not encryption: it keeps out a casual look, not someone who can read the browser's storage.
- The collection, its photographs and papers, and the sync key are kept in this browser's storage as they are. Sync encrypts what it sends; nothing is encrypted on the device.
- So anyone who can read the browser's storage, through its developer tools, the device's files or a copy of them, can read the collection with or without the passcode.
- The passcode is kept on this device only, as a salted PBKDF2-SHA-256 hash of 210,000 rounds. It is never synced and never in a backup, so each device has its own or none.
- It asks when the app opens in a new tab or window, and again once the app has been out of sight for the time set: at once, or after 1, 5 or 15 minutes. A reload of an unlocked tab stays unlocked.
- After five wrong passcodes it waits 30 seconds. The about pages are never behind it.
- If you forget the passcode, this device's sync key opens the app, and so does a backup file of this collection, read on the device and not restored. Either takes the passcode off, to be set again in Settings.
- So it can be set only while sync is on or after a backup made in the last day. With neither the key nor a file, clearing the site's data in the browser takes the lock off, and the collection on this device with it.
- The example collection has no lock.

## /about/formats

### 3. accession (h3 `#f-accession`), after `olderKind`

- `season` (winter, summer, none): the plant's growing season, as you set or confirmed it on its page. `restMonths`: the months, 1 to 12, it rests in, at most eleven; none for a plant with no rest.
- `seasonFrom` (rain, cool, spread, grower): what you confirmed the season from. That is the species page's rain rule, its temperature rule or its spread rain, or your own setting.
- A season of another word, or months that are not whole numbers from 1 to 12, or all twelve, is read as no season set, and the plant follows its space. Absent on a plant never given one.

### 4. photo (h3 `#f-photo`), after the pixels' line

- A paper kept with a plant is a photo record too, with `acc`, `d` (the day it was added), `bytes` (the file's size), `sha` and `caption`.
- It has three fields a photograph never has. `mime` is the file's type (application/pdf, image/jpeg or image/png), read from its first bytes. `fileName` is its name as added, at most 120 characters. `doc` (cites, phyto, invoice, other) is what it is.
- A paper has no `w` or `h`. So a version made before papers keeps it as a record waiting for fields, never shows it as a photograph, and still syncs and backs up its bytes.
- Its file is stored under the record's id behind a JPEG of one grey pixel, 159 bytes, and the thumbnail is that JPEG alone. Every version's backup reader takes both for JPEGs, and this one reads the file whole after the first 159 bytes. Its `sha` is of the stored bytes.

### 5. The backup's spreadsheets (h3 `#sheets`), after the first line

- Its columns, in order: number, species, cultivar, kind, parentage, name as received, field number, provenance, status, location, acquired, from, lot or reference, form, price, sowing and notes.
- Then GBIF key, water every (days), older name of, growing season, rest months (as Jun Jul Aug), season from, photographs (a count) and papers (each as its kind and file name, joined by semicolons). The id is last.
- The import reads the columns before them back by their heads, but for sowing, and names the others apart without adding them to a plant's notes.

### 6. The label's QR code (h3 `#label`): the first line changed, one added

Old (made untrue by F10): "**The label's QR code** is the plant's address, `/plants/<identity>#s=<species>&n=<name as printed>`."

New:

- The label's QR code is the plant's address by its number, `/plants/<number>#s=<species>&n=<name as printed>`, the number escaped as an address needs. On label tape it is the address alone, `/plants/<number>`, so the code is small enough to read at 9 mm. A batch's is `/propagation/<number>`.
- A plant's page finds a plant by its number as by its identity. A code printed before this carries the identity, `/plants/<identity>#…`, and opens the plant as it did.

## Proposed, not built: the collection encrypted on the device with a passcode-derived key

Feasible without risking data, but a large change, so it is a proposal only (the brief's F12):

- A random 256-bit data key encrypts each change's `value` and each photograph's and paper's bytes before they reach
  IndexedDB (`vault.ts`: the change store, the photo store, the fold snapshot, the outbox needs nothing). `t`, `kind`,
  `id` and `field` stay plain, so the indexes, the order of arrival and the fold's bookkeeping work as now.
- The data key is wrapped twice: under a key derived from the passcode (PBKDF2 or Argon2id), and under a key derived from
  the sync key (HKDF, as the vault's keys are now) where sync is on. Forgetting the passcode then loses nothing on a
  device that syncs; on one that does not, a backup file is the only way back, and the setting would say so before it
  is turned on.
- The sync key itself, kept in `meta` in the clear today, would be kept encrypted under the data key.
- Every read path decrypts (`allChanges`, `changesByKeys`, `changesOf`, `readFold`, `getPhotoBlobs`), so each tab needs
  the data key in memory after unlock, handed to new tabs over a BroadcastChannel after their own unlock, never stored.
- Turning it on rewrites the whole vault: done through the staging database, as Replace does, so a cut-off midway
  resumes rather than leaves half a vault. `DB_V` and `FOLD_RULES` move (the snapshot is encrypted too).
- Backups stay plain unless a passcode-protected backup format is added (it would need a new manifest version that older
  builds refuse in words, as format 3 is refused now).
