# Review round seventeen: two reviews of `1fc23f3`, triaged

Two reviews of the round-sixteen build arrived together: the ninth pass from the second reviewer (four findings, one reproduced with a real IndexedDB, and the Lighthouse and axe runs), and the first reviewer's round seventeen, three readers wide again, with thirteen findings, one of them live-breaking and caused by round sixteen. Every finding was checked in the source and is real; all seventeen are done, and both of the first reviewer's design notes are taken. "A-n" is the second reviewer's numbering, "B-n" the first's; where both found the same thing (A-1 and B-4, A-2 and B-3, A-4 and B-5) it is one item here.

## Live

1. **The species picker's name lookup returned 500 for any name looked up before** (B-1; the reviewer's one thing to fix, and mine: round sixteen's headers hook). The names route returns its edge-cache hit as it is, a cached response's headers are immutable in Workers, the hook's `set` threw, and SvelteKit answered 500 from the second lookup of any name on; the picker then swallowed the failure and offered only the catalogue's own matches, so a grower adding a species outside the 8,947 found no name and no sign anything had failed. Neither suite could see it, since neither runs the real edge cache. Fixed in `src/hooks.server.ts` (the hook works on a copy of the response, `new Response(res.body, res)`) and in the names route (the hit is returned as a copy too); and the picker now says under the field that the name service did not answer and that a name typed in full is kept as typed and checked later, which is the reviewer's design note. Unit test on the hook with a response whose headers throw on `set`; it fails against the old hook.

## Lost or wrong data

2. **A run of a vault this device had left could still empty the new vault's outbox, or fold the old vault's batch into the new log** (A-1, B-4; a fault in round sixteen's fix, which checked the stored key and then wrote in another transaction). The check now lives inside the write: `outboxAck(stamps, key)` reads the stored sync record and deletes in one transaction, and a server-side fold (`appendChanges(…, requireKey)`, through `collection.ingest`) does the same, so a tab that stopped syncing or moved to another vault between a read and a write can no longer be undone by the other tab's run. A stale run also no longer writes its progress text or reaches for the engine's keys: each step is taken only while the run's meta is still the device's, and the keys are kept beside the meta, not on the engine, so the "Receiving 1 of 1…" that B-4 found stuck for good, with sync never running again until a reload, cannot be left behind. Vault tests on a real in-memory IndexedDB (the guarded ack and the guarded write refuse and touch nothing); engine tests for both interleavings and for the stale run that wakes after the new vault's first run, which fails against the old code exactly as the reviewer described.
3. **A change the vault displaced under its stamp stayed on the screen, and other tabs never saw the swap** (A-2, B-3; round sixteen's item 4 claimed the screen and the disk agreed, and they did not in this arrival order). The incremental fold has no inverse for a displaced change; a grower editing the ghost would write a fragment of a record the disk did not have. The store now reports what it replaced, the collection rebuilds its fold from the log whenever that is not empty, and a `refold` notice makes every other tab do the same, so the ghost leaves everywhere without a reload. Two changes under one stamp in one batch (a hand-made import file) are reduced to one by rank before anything is written, rather than the last `put` winning. Test on the real vault with the real collection: the ghost is gone after the second ingest; a batch with two under one stamp keeps the higher.
4. **The v2 fallback event id collided with real v2 ids** (B-2; a fault in round sixteen's item 4). Round sixteen named an id-less event `e<n>`, the namespace of v2's own `e1`, `e2`…, so a plant with one id-less event beside `e1` lost the acquire event, overwritten field by field, the same way on every device. The fallback is `#<n>`, which no v2 id can be. Import test with the mixed plant: three events survive, the acquire under its own id.
5. **A slow species lookup silently overwrote the bench the grower chose on the add form** (A-3; reproduced in both of the reviewer's full journeys: ten plants meant for three benches, six and seven of them in one). The form was interactive while it awaited the reference's answer, and the last-used place was applied afterwards, over whatever had been chosen. The default is applied right after the collection loads, before the reference is asked anything, and only to a place not yet chosen by hand. e2e with the sheet request delayed: the grower's choice survives the answer and the plant is filed under it.

## The build, and how the pages read it

6. **A refused range still read as "no range exists", and the in-range counts were still published** (A-4, B-5; not in today's corpus). Round sixteen's refused-range branch sat behind the "no verified native range" branch and could not be reached; the "Wild records" fact said "352 in range", the map caption "inside the range", the CSV export's `in_range_records` 352, and the country-level sentence claimed a map marker the build does not make. The refused branch comes first now; the fact says "352 georeferenced · 0 open · range not tested", the caption says the records were not tested against the stated range, the export leaves the column blank, and the country-level sentence says what is derived without a range (nothing) and what the map shows.
7. **A suffixed homonym's page, compare, follow and notes pointed at the other species** (B-6; not in today's corpus, 0 suffixed slugs). Both homonyms' files carry the plain slug, and the page used the file's slug for every link and key. The species and compare pages now use the slug they were asked for, which is the index's; the server's fallback sheet derivation files each sheet under the index slug as the build's bucket files do; and `sheetForName` looks for `<slug>-<key>` when the plain slug answers with another key, before it "repairs" a plant's key to the other species'.
8. **A refused NASA POWER source printed a warmer, unmarked night on the label** (B-7; not in today's corpus). The care line fell back to the CHELSA mean night as "hab. night", a different and warmer quantity, with nothing to say so; a `--quick` build's skipped extremes read as "no daily extremes are on file". With the source refused the night is left off the label and the labels page counts those; skipped extremes read as "not asked for when this page was built" on the sheet, the glance and compare. Note test.
9. **Two credit gaps in the build** (B-13). An empty `creator` beside a `rightsHolder` published ", CC BY, via GBIF" (the filter used `||`, the credit `??`); both use `||` now. The prune left a dossier's photo sources marked `ok` after emptying it, so a fill would not look again; the pass now reopens the sources of any dossier it leaves without a photograph, where a source had answered, and rewords the 1,018 CC0 credits that read "unknown" to "author not stated". The 53 species round sixteen's prune emptied all have iNaturalist and Commons recorded as having nothing for them, so `docs/DEPLOY.md` no longer says a fill will give them a photograph. The pass is run once more.

## What leaves the device

10. **The old site-wide hemisphere cookie was still sent everywhere** (B-8; seen live). Round sixteen expired the `path=/` copy only when Settings was next saved. The site store expires it on every load and writes the path-scoped copies then, so no device carries the old one past its next page load.
11. **The photo switch did not reach other tabs, and the plants-list offer's "Nothing is sent to Cultifolio" was not quite right** (B-9). The preference store listens for the storage event, so a tab switched off in Settings stops fetching without a reload; the plants-list offer says that this site is asked for the hash buckets of the species, as the plant pages ask; `/about/how` names the in-page offer beside Settings.
12. **The entries route read the index and the corpus id in two loads** (B-10; the case round sixteen's item 12 closed, reopened across the cache's minute). One load returns both.

## Confusing and cosmetic

14. **Several plants with a brought number** (B-12). Only the first takes the brought number; the subline now says so ("The first will be numbered 2025-0100, the rest from 2026-0007") instead of naming the minted number alone.
