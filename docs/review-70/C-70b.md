## What changed

1. **"Media from GBIF" is no longer a kind.** It says where a file came from, not what it shows. Herbarium imports are still caught by their specimen categories and titles.
   - **Copies:** a Commons file whose name ends with a number of six to nine digits after a space is read as a copy of that iNaturalist photograph (iNaturalist2Commons names files this way). If the dossier holds that photograph from iNaturalist or GBIF, the copy is shown and counted once (`commonsInatCopy`, in `shownPhotos`). A Flickr number in brackets is not read.
   - **How much this catches:** of the 62 GBIF imports, 7 carry such a number and 6 of those duplicate a photograph the dossier holds (Agave eggersiana, A. nizandensis, Babiana planifolia, Yucca intermedia, Sinningia aghensis, Drosera ultramafica).
   - **What it misses:** files named "<name> 1.jpg" ("Melocactus paucispinus 1") carry no number, and no cheap rule ties them to a photograph. They may show twice where the page also holds the iNaturalist original.
2. **The group rule counts species of the reference.**
   - Each category is mapped to the species it names. The species' own names (B's rule through `olderNameIsOwn`, compared by `spellingKey`) and its varieties count as the species itself ("Lithops schwantesii subsp. schwantesii var. marthae"). A variety of another species counts as that species. Names the reference files under one key count once. A name the reference does not hold, and that is not this species' own, counts as itself.
   - Category names now parse with two ranks.
   - "bonsai" is not an epithet, so "Cyphostemma bonsai" and "Zanthoxylum bonsai" are not species.
   - **Departure:** Commons' categories show that "Aztekium hintonii 5" is filed under Selaginella gypsophila, a second plant, and "Dinnerplate Aralia (Polyscias balfouriana)" under Polyscias pinnata. These are not two names of one plant, as the brief supposed, so the mapping alone keeps them hidden. I added one rule: a file is no group when its title names this species and none of the other species' epithets. Under this rule a plant visible beside the subject does not hide it. "Haworthias blackburniae y wittebergensis" still names two, and the true groups' titles name none (Liberec, Balchik, Knersvlakte, "Haworthia (5511309933)", Agave20140104, Tourcoing, Gent, Roscoff, Zürich "Tiefland", "The three amigos", Stellenbosch, "Semillero de Lithops").
   - The same rule shows two files whose title names the species while Commons also files other plants in them: Adenia globosa at the Wilhelma (with Bijlia and Curio) and "Aizoaceae Gibbaeum Dispar" (with Aloinopsis).
3. **Animals.** An animal's category hides a file only when its title does not name the species under one of its own names. It is read by name or by a zoological family ending in -idae: Formicidae, Tabanidae, Papilionidae, Tipulidae.
4. **"Unidentified"** is read only of a plant. That means "Unidentified" followed by plants, flowers, succulents, cacti or a plant group (-ales, -aceae, Angiosperms), or by a genus of the species or of the reference. "Unidentified year in Donetsk" is not about the plant.
   - "Ab plant 1310" and "1313" (Kroenleinia grusonii) and "Ab plant 846", "854" and "862" (Haworthiopsis attenuata) are shown. No title rule hides them, contrary to the brief. Commons files them under the species and "Potted plants in Donetsk", and 846's description says "Haworthiopsis attenuata in cultivation".
5. **`spellingKey`, for A:** a genitive in -ai is read as -ae (Rec. 60C), so kanehirai and kanehirae are one name. Over the index this joins no two accepted names of one genus.

## Needs

**P, `/about/how`:** replace "A Commons file is shown only as … or another plant." with:

"A Commons file is shown only as a JPEG or WebP photograph whose name, and its categories as Commons gave them to the build or to the fill that asks for them, mark it as none of these: a map; a pressed, mounted or museum specimen (a living plant in a garden is shown, whatever its category calls it, and where Commons got a file, GBIF or iNaturalist, is not read); a plate, drawing or other printed illustration, or a paper's figure; seeds (a pod on the plant is the plant), resin, a stamp, a book or a label; an animal, unless the name names the species (a flower with an insect on it is the plant); a plant Commons calls unidentified; a hybrid ("hybrid" in its name, or the sign); a plant named "aff." or "cf."; a photograph Commons files under two or more species, counting the species' own names and its varieties as the species, unless its name names this species and none of the others; one whose description strikes out a name; or another plant. A Commons copy of an iNaturalist photograph the page shows, known by the photograph's number at the end of its name, is shown once."

In the same paragraph, change "("gender endings, hyphens and spaces, "ae" or "oe" for "e", "ii" for "i")" to "(gender endings, an adjective in -er that keeps its e ("stolonifer", "stolonifera"), hyphens and spaces, "ae" or "oe" for "e", "ii" for "i", "ai" for "ae")". Make the same change in the Wikipedia sentence's "(a gender ending, a hyphen, "ae" for "e", "ii" for "i")". Both read `spellingKey`.
