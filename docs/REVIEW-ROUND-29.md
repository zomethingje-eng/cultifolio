# Review round twenty-nine: two reviews of `65a31fa`, triaged

The first reviewer's round twenty-eight (a photo-heavy phone collector: 120 plants and 603 phone photographs at 390 px with the CPU slowed four times, an adversarial pass over each round-twenty-eight claim, and the five security lows named at last) and the second reviewer's twentieth pass (three findings; Lighthouse 95–100 on every page and view, zero axe violations, a 2.1-minute clean run of the browser suite). Every finding was checked in the source or reproduced. All are real. All but two are done; those two are at the end with why. "R1-n" is the first reviewer's; "R2-n" the second's.

## Records lost or wrong

1. **The photo viewer removed a different photograph after a date edit** (R1-1; the reviewer's one thing to fix). The viewer showed `photos[index]`; saving a date re-sorts the set and the index stayed, so Remove took the neighbour, and the pixels went at once with no way back. The viewer holds the photograph by id and the index follows it; a removal returns the way back (the pixels are read before they are deleted and held by the Undo for its eight seconds), the viewer closes so the Undo is reachable, and Undo restores the record, the cover and the pixels. e2e: three photographs, the first dated back to 2019 (it sorts last, the viewer says "3 of 3"), Remove takes that one, Undo brings it back, and it is still there after a reload.
2. **The type check refused whole backups and batches that older builds wrote** (R1-2, reproduced for a backup). Until round twenty-eight the v2 importer wrote a numeric price through, so a device that imported one holds a number in its log and writes it into every backup; round twenty-eight's check then refused that device's own backup after a wipe, and a peer quarantined the batch. Now a finite number in a text field becomes its text, at three places: as the log is read from the vault (so the fold, a backup and a push all see text, and the value stops travelling), in a backup as it is opened, and in a pulled batch. A value of a type its field never takes is left out on its own and named (the backup preview says "1 change in the file cannot be read and is left out (change 18: …)"; a batch logs it), and the rest is folded; only structure (a bad stamp, an unknown kind, a reserved name) still refuses the whole, since that is a damaged or foreign list. Unit tests for each path.
3. **Undo of a removal could leave two live plants on one number** (R1-3; reproduced at unit level with two stores, as the reviewer did). The ledger is per device; another device minting the same number offline is caught by the repair, which skips removed plants, so a plant restored after the merge shared its number. The repair runs after every restore of a plant, and the Undo toast opens the plant by the number it holds afterwards. Unit test: A removes 2026-0007, merges B's 2026-0007, restores; the two are 0007 and 0008.
4. **A full device failed without a word** (R1-4). Chrome's `QuotaExceededError` has an empty message and the photo control showed `err.message`. A storage failure is named now ("This device is out of space for the collection: 412 MB in use of the 480 MB the browser allows this site…"), on the photo control (which stops after the first, since the rest would fail the same way) and on the backup page; a restore that fails part way takes out the pixels it had written, so a device is not left holding bytes no record names.

## Propagation

5. **Two date guards could be walked around** (R1-5). The count guard judged totals, so a count could go while a potting rested on it by date, and a potting of 8 dated after a count of 1 passed. What was in the pot on a day is one figure now (the latest count up to that day, less what had been potted or lost by then); a potting or a loss must fit it on its own day ("Only 1 was in the pot on 2026-09-18 by the counts recorded"), and a count can go only if every later potting and loss still fits without it. The batch edit form judges only what the edit typed, as the plant form does since round twenty-eight: a batch whose stored date is wrong can still have its medium edited. e2e for all three.

## Import and sync

6. **The v2 importer still passed some raw values and could throw** (R1-8). Every text field goes through the importer's own rule now (a number becomes text, anything else is dropped): the name, an event's date, a bench's id and name, an overlay's name and notes; a bench with a numeric id no longer throws in `slugify`. A method word is taken wherever it sits in v2's label ("Leaf cuttings" is leaf, not seed) and a provenance outside the five is dropped.
7. **`cutBefore` still split the pair it was changed for** (R1-9). It moved a cut only when the change at the cut was itself the base. It now looks a few steps past the cut for a base whose own notes change sits before it, and moves before that; a pair that starts the batch is kept whole by cutting after it; the refusal resplit uses it too. Unit tests for the cut between, the pair at the start, and a foreign edit between.
8. **Words the types do not know** (R1-13). A status of "banana" hid a plant from every list but All and a method the app does not have rendered as seed. The log refuses a value outside the union for a plant's or batch's status, provenance and name kind, a batch's method, a photograph's date source and a place's kind, one change at a time as in 2; an event's type is left open on purpose, so a newer build's new type still reaches an older device. A test holds the lists to the unions in `types.ts`. Also: a first sync says "Sending photo 592 of 598".
9. **Adding several photographs lost its progress** (R1-12). The first one stored unmounted the control, taking "Storing 2 of 5" and any error with it. The control says when it starts and the page keeps it on screen from then. e2e.
10. **The species picker asked twice when blurred mid-request** (R2-3, reproduced by the reviewer with an 800 ms delay). Round twenty-eight cached the answer; the request itself is cached now, so a blur during the search reads the same promise. A failed request is forgotten.

## The site's claims and the deploy

11. **Request logging was still on** (R1-6, R2-1). The commit that said it turned observability off changed only `package.json` and the lockfile: the replace I gave for `wrangler.jsonc` matched a one-line form and the file's block spans three lines. The file is the deployer's and is not in this round's files; the one-line PowerShell edit that handles either layout is in the message with this round, and `Select-String` shows the result before the deploy.
12. **The plants-only name filter is proved on the deployed Worker** (R2-2). The live check's genus rotates by the minute and tests only that the genus is named somewhere, so an older Worker could pass. A fixed `gaster` assertion is in the check now: Gasteria present, no Curculionidae, Gasterosteidae or Scombridae. (The reviewer's live reproduction was against the Worker before `65a31fa` was deployed; the deploy after it passed the rotating check without proving the filter, which is what this closes.)
13. **The vault-full notice gave advice that did not work** (R1-7). Nothing on the server is rewritten or deleted, so "Delete photographs" freed nothing. The notice says so and says what does work (back up, set up a new vault). A photo tombstone that frees the object server-side and on peers is a feature for later.

## Robustness and security (R1-10)

14. **Attribute injection in the still map.** `esc` did not escape `"` and the title lands in `aria-label`. It does now.
15. **Prototype field names.** A field named `__proto__`, `constructor` or anything on `Object.prototype` is refused whatever its value, and the type table is read with `Object.hasOwn`. Test.
16. **A zip made to inflate to gigabytes** froze the page for twelve seconds and was then accepted. The reader takes only the entries a backup has (the three JSON files, the two sheets, a photograph or its thumbnail) and refuses any entry declared over 256 MB at the table of contents, before a byte is inflated. Test.
17. **Dossier URLs.** Every address a species page turns into a link (a photograph's page, a summary's, a paper's, the links row) is scrubbed to web addresses when the dossier is parsed; a photograph whose own address is not one is dropped.
18. **The CSV guard** looked at the first character only and prefixed numbers. It judges text only, after leading spaces, the full-width signs included; a bottom heat of −5 is written as -5. Test.
19. **The fold's tie key** for a repeated removal stamp used a space where every other key uses NUL, so that lookup never matched. Fixed; the fold tests hold.
20. **The backup writer held three copies of the file** (R1-11). Six hundred photographs made a 128 MB zip with a 574 MB peak: every photograph, the zip, and a copy of it. The writer streams now, one entry at a time, and keeps the zip as the pieces fflate produces; the page makes the file a Blob of them, so nothing is held twice. The reader still inflates the whole zip at once; streaming it is the next step if a phone kills a restore.

## Checked and not taken

**`storeOnce` without a conditional put** (R1-10). Real: a head then a put, so two pushes of one key racing can overwrite. The fix is R2's `onlyIf`, and the wildcard form is not something I can try against a real bucket from here; the harm is confined to the key holder's own vault (a device racing itself), and the object is ciphertext either way. It goes in the next round with a probe on the deployed bucket. **Removing the "Marked done" line leaves the status** (from round twenty-eight): by design, as said then.
