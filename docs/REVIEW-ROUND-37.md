# Review round thirty-seven: two reviews of the deployed `3a4c444`, triaged

The first reviewer's round thirty-two (nine findings and three small ones, in depth, with reproductions) and the second reviewer's round twenty-four (four findings and a design note, with fresh Lighthouse runs). Both reviewed what rounds thirty-four and thirty-five deployed. Every finding was checked in the source or reproduced. "R1-n" is the first reviewer's; "R2-n" the second's. The first reviewer's one thing to fix is the cold floor, and it is right: it was the one place the site erred toward a dead plant.

## The collection

1. **A backup, and sync, left out the pixels of a waiting photograph** (R1-1, reproduced). `buildBackup` read `live()`, which since round thirty-three means complete records only, so a photograph whose size sat in a set-aside batch was neither written to the zip nor named under `photosMissing`; "Replace this device with the file" then lost pixels that round thirty-five had kept on purpose. The push did the same through `collectionPhotos()`, and the pull did not ask for them. A new `known()` (not removed, whole or waiting) is what the backup writer, `photosWithoutPixels`, the push and the pull go by; the store's `knownPhotos()` carries the `sha` the pull checks. Test: a waiting photograph travels with its pixels, is counted, and is named as missing when they are not there.
2. **A plant that waits for a field was told it was removed** (R1-4, reproduced). `/plants/A-2` fell through to "given to a plant since removed; the number stays reserved", because the number was taken and the record was not removed. The page now says the record is here and which field it has no value for (its name, its status), that a newer build's batch may still bring it or the file never had it, and, when the status is the only thing missing, offers one button that marks it as growing. The plants page said "set aside on Sync" to a grower who does not sync; it says that only while Sync holds a set-aside batch, and otherwise that the file never had the field or the batch has not arrived. The restore preview names the waiting records (up to five, "plant 2026-0003, batch Aloe"), not only their count.
3. **The sheet in a backup disagreed with the app about a plant's place** (R2-1, reproduced). `locPath` returned an empty cell as soon as it reached an incomplete or removed place, where the store's `rawParent` walks past it to the nearest whole one: a plant the app shows in House had a blank location in `plants.csv` of the same file. It walks past now, with the cycle guard. Tests for a plant and a batch under an incomplete place, and a plant under a removed one.
4. **The backup reader could be made to use a gigabyte by one stored block** (R1-5; reproduced from the reviewer's description). The size check summed `originalSize`, but fflate copies a stored entry by its compressed size: a table of contents whose hundred entries all point at one 1 MB stored block and each declare one byte passed the sum and cost a copy of the block apiece. The larger of the two sizes counts, and a stored entry whose two sizes differ is refused as not ours. The test builds that zip by hand (one stored entry, its central-directory record repeated under a hundred names, every record at the same offset) and fails against the old reader.
5. **A vegetative method saved a null provenance** (R1 small). Round thirty-five's "leave unset what the record never had" rule ran before the method rule; a batch with no provenance switched to cuttings wrote `null` instead of `veg`. The method decides first.

## The cold floor

6. **The figure that replaced a sea cell's night was warmer than it** (R1-2, the reviewer's one thing to fix; R1-3). Round thirty-five set aside extremes read at a cell under half land and headed the range's coldest monthly mean night "Cold floor" in their place. A month's mean of its lows is above its coldest nights by construction, and a sea-moderated cell is warmer at night than the land beside it, so Dudleya brittonii read "Cold floor 9.6 °C" over a notice saying the cell gave 3.9 °C. The mean night is now named as what it is wherever it stands in for a floor, and that is every status without extremes, not only the sea's: the species card is headed "Coldest mean night" and its line says it is not a floor and why; the sheet's temperature row says "Cold floor: none read. The nearest figure is …, a month's mean night, warmer than the nights a floor is read from"; compare says "not a floor"; the label's care line prints no habitat night for the sea status (as it already did for a refusal), and the labels page counts those; the plant page's night line says the extremes were read at a sea cell and set aside; the sea notice says no floor is read. The corpus export writes no `extreme_min_p01_c` for a cell under half land. The figure the rule settles on is unchanged (nothing compares against it); only what it is called. Tests over the sea, none and refused statuses, and the label.
7. **Plant pages and labels still read the sea figure** (R1-3). The sheet buckets were built before the rule and carry raw extremes, and `sheetOf` reads a parsed dossier, which the rule is applied to, so the `--index` corpus step rewrites them with the `sea` status and no extremes. This round needs that step (and the two `rclone` copies), not a rederive; the deploy note says so.

## The reference

8. **The carried-row chain in the files** (R1-6). Round thirty-five stopped the builder adding to a chain and left the chains already in the files, nine deep on Albuca yerburyi. `unchain()` cuts a chain to the build that asked and the reason that build gave; applied as a dossier is read (so the live pages are right on deploy) and by the builder (so the files stop carrying it). Test.
9. **"f", "var", "ssp" dropped while being typed** (R1-7, reproduced). A rank marker is left out of the query only when another word follows it; a trailing one is tried as a word first ("aloe var" finds variegata, "f" finds Ferocactus) and dropped when nothing starts with it ("haworthia pumila var" still finds Tulista pumila). Tests.
10. **A variety of a listed older name arrived with no line** (R1-8). `haworthia-attenuata-var-radula` reached Haworthiopsis attenuata, whose record lists Haworthia attenuata and not that variety of it, so the guard found nothing to vouch for. A trinomial whose binomial is a listed older name is shown as "a variety under an older name of this species": what the record holds, said as the record holds it. A name the record does not hold is still not printed. E2e test.
11. **"northand", and the boundary** (R2-2, reproduced). The block's boundary comment ate the space before "and" in the server-rendered sentence. The clause is one string now, and the boundary reads "at least 10°", as the split counts it (`>= 10`). A fixture carries a split, and an e2e test reads the server's HTML before hydration.
12. **The Rhipsalis count** (R1-9). The live dossier predates the `equatorial` field, and the page's fallback is `cells − north`, which for that corpus is the equatorial count by construction (`cells` is what the envelope reads: the used side's cells and the equatorial ones). That the south count is also 138 is a coincidence the next rederive will settle by writing the field; the fallback stays, since it is the record's own arithmetic and not a guess.
