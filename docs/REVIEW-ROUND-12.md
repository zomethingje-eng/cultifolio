# Review round twelve: two reviews of `1bd000e`, triaged

Two reviews of the round-eleven build arrived together: the round-four pass from the second reviewer (three findings, all on what leaves the device and what the sheet route costs) and a round-twelve pass from the first reviewer, who went wider than the changes with two further readers on sync and on the figures (ten findings, one check that came out clean: 160 live species compared across the card, the note, the plant page and the label in both unit systems, 0 differences). Every finding was checked in the source; all thirteen were real. "A-n" is the second reviewer's numbering, "B-n" the first's. In the order of what they cost a grower.

## Data lost or wrong

1. **A batch whose download broke off midway was set aside for good** (B-2; the first reviewer's one thing to fix). `res.arrayBuffer()` ran inside the try block that quarantines unreadable batches, so a connection that dropped after the 200 header was recorded as bad bytes, the key went into `have`, the cursor moved past it, and the changes in it never reached the device. Photographs the same. A batch a newer build wrote (`v !== 1`) was set aside permanently too, even after the device updated, and a 429 on one batch or photo threw a plain error instead of the server's `Retry-After`. Fixed in `src/lib/sync/engine.svelte.ts`: the body is read whole before anything is judged, so a dropped connection stops the run and the batch is fetched again next run; only bytes that arrived whole and cannot be opened, parsed or validated are set aside; each quarantine entry records the build that failed to read it, and a later build drops those entries from the quarantine and from `have` and reads them again; 429 on a batch or a photo is `limited()`. Engine tests: a body that errors mid-stream (a `ReadableStream` that enqueues then errors) leaves nothing quarantined and the next run brings the record; a 429 carries its `retryAfterMs`; an entry marked by an older build is fetched again by this one.
2. **One commit after a fast-clock episode could give two fields one stamp, and the second overwrote the first on disk** (B-4). `stampPast` moved each field past its own previous stamp; two fields whose previous stamps differed only by writer landed on the same `hlcAfter` value, and the `changes` store is keyed by stamp. The screen showed both edits; the vault and every other device had one. Fixed: within a commit, a stamp already given out is stepped again (only an actual collision moves; a field left at real time keeps its stamp, which round eight's rule requires), and a local commit stores with `add`, so a repeat stamp fails loudly rather than replacing. Store test: notes and price edited together after a correction, every change under its own key, both values after a reload; the test fails against the old line.
3. **The duplicate-number repair could give one plant two numbers under one stamp** (B-3). The repair's stamp was a function of the record alone, the number it chose depended on how much of the log the device held, and it ran after every pulled batch; two devices repairing at different points in a pull wrote different numbers with the same stamp, and `apply()` kept whichever arrived first while IndexedDB kept the last. Fixed three ways: the chosen number is in the stamp's tag, so different repairs are different changes and the ordinary merge settles them the same way everywhere; the repair runs once at the end of a pull (`collection.repairNumbers()`), not per batch; and `apply()` breaks a tie between two changes under one stamp by value, so the fold is order-independent even then. Store test: two devices repair the same duplicate to different numbers (one log short a batch), the stamps differ, and after the logs meet both devices hold the same numbers, all distinct, byte-identical logs.
4. **Labels printed some southern species' season six months off** (B-1). The labels passed the marker's latitude to the care line and nothing when there was no marker (24 of 1,469 species with a climate in the seven buckets the reviewer sampled, 7 of them southern); the species page and the plant page fell back to the typical cell's latitude. The sheet now carries `habitatLat` (the marker's, else the cell's) and the labels and the plant page read it. Unit test on `sheetOf` with the marker removed: the latitude is the cell's, southern, and the care line differs from the one printed without it.
5. **A cleared number field became `null`, and on the new-batch form it blocked the save** (B-5). Svelte 5 binds an emptied `type="number"` input to `null`; the checks tested for `''`. "null °F is colder than no heat at all" on `/sowings/new`; the batch's Edit form saved a cleared heat as 0 °C and never applied the 5–45 °C check; a cleared measurement box recorded 0 and bent the growth figure. Fixed: `blank()` / `numberOrNull()` in `src/lib/core/units.ts` treat `''`, `null` and NaN as not given; one `bottomHeat()` check serves both sowing forms (the Edit form now refuses 77 °C too, with the field marked); the measurement inputs skip a blank; the germination and loss counts accept a cleared box.
6. **v2 imports and restores bypassed the "numbers never reused" ledger** (B-6). The importer used the number as the record's id and never wrote the `acc`/`no` field the ledger reads; the staging copy of a restore never touched the ledger. Import this year's plants from v2, replace from a backup taken before that, and the next plant was 2026-0001 again. Fixed: the importer writes the number as a field on every plant and batch, tombstones included (a removed plant's number stays issued), and `copyStagingIn` goes through `storeIn`, which puts the numbers it carries on the ledger. Import test on the fields.

## What leaves the device

7. **A private page fetched the species' photograph from iNaturalist, which then knew the species** (A-1; the second reviewer's one thing to fix). The plant page's hero, the plants list's thumbnails, the batch page's picture and the front page's own tiles loaded the reference's photograph from the image host, exact species path and reader's address, under a page that said the collection never leaves the device. Fixed with a preference, off by default (`src/lib/ui/prefs.svelte.ts`, on `/settings`): off, those pages ask no outside host for anything and show the grower's own photograph or a placeholder (an own tile says "photograph on the species page", not "no open photograph on file", which would be false); on, the photograph comes straight from the image host and Settings says what that host then sees. Species pages are unaffected: their photograph is what the visitor came for. e2e: a grower's plant page, list and front page make no request outside the origin, and the front page fetches nothing about the plant grown (its rows and featured strip are the public catalogue); switched on, the plant page fetches that species' photograph and still sends this server no key or name.
8. **The hash-bucket lookups contradicted the categorical wording** (A-2). The bucket channel was disclosed on `/about/formats` and denied by "your collection never leaves your device" on `/about/how`, in the README and in the Show HN draft. The wording now matches the behaviour everywhere: the records are stored here and sent nowhere unless sync is on, and then encrypted; the one thing the server sees from a grower's own pages is which of 32 buckets they asked for, one in about 280; those pages ask no third-party host for anything by default. `/about/how` links to the rule.

## Cost and staleness

9. **A cold sheet bucket took seconds to over a minute, nothing kept it but one Worker instance, and one request could drive the whole corpus worth of reads** (A-3, B-8). Measured by the reviewer: 8.1 s for one cold bucket, 73.7 s for twelve; no `cf-cache-status`, because a Worker's response is not cached by the edge unless the Worker puts it there, so round ten's "the edge keeps each bucket a day" was not happening, and this file says so. Fixed in three layers. The corpus build now writes the 32 bucket files (`npm run dossier -- --index` → `static/s/v2/sheets/00.json…1f.json`, uploaded with the corpus), and `sheetsIn` serves one object read; derivation from the dossiers remains only for a corpus uploaded without them and the fixtures. The route puts each bucket in the edge cache under its own canonical key (`/api/sheets?b=03&c=<corpus>`), whatever the request's grouping, and the client asks one bucket per request so the URLs repeat across devices. And a `sheets` rate bucket (40 derived buckets per address per ten minutes) counts only what the edge did not hold; at most four buckets a request. Measured on the corpus build with the files: a cold bucket 20–30 ms. Route tests: the cap, the canonical put, a cached bucket answered without derivation, the file served when present, the limit on derived buckets only.
10. **After a corpus refresh, plant pages and labels kept the old figures while the species page showed the new ones** (B-7). The worker served the reference files cache-first "for the build", but a corpus refresh is an upload with no deploy. Fixed with a corpus id: `/api/corpus` (never cached) reports the index object's etag; the client puts it on every reference request (`?c=`), remembering the last one for offline use; a refresh is a new URL for the edge, the worker and the browser at once. `docs/DEPLOY.md` says to upload the dossiers and sheet files before `index.json` (two `rclone copy` runs), so no device sees the new id before the files behind it are there.
11. **The bench's frost watch said "Frost forecast" for a night above freezing and "All clear" when the alerts were not checked** (B-9). With a floor set, any night below it was level `frost`; an NWS warning was dropped once a floor was set; the bench had no `alertsStatus` and said "All clear" when the service had not answered, which the frost page, from the same answer, refused to say; a 2.5 °C floor printed as "3 °C". Fixed: a night below the floor is "Below the floor" (its own level and pill), a warning in force is said whatever the floor, "Alerts not checked" is said on the bench as on the frost page, and the floor prints at one decimal. e2e with a stubbed forecast.
12. **The sync bookkeeping grew without limit, and a vault stopped listing at 20,000 batches** (B-10). `have` held every batch key the device ever saw and the whole meta record was rewritten after every listed batch. Fixed on the client: `have` is pruned to keys whose arrival is within ten overlap windows of the cursor (a key listed again after that is folded again, which the log makes harmless), and the meta is written once per page. On the server the listing bound is raised from 20 to 50 pages (50,000 batches: a grower who syncs with changes five times a day for twenty-five years); the walk is still the whole prefix per pull, because R2 lists in key order and the client needs arrival order. The structural fix, naming stored objects by arrival so the list can start at the cursor, is a wire change for another round and is noted in `src/lib/server/sync.ts`.
