# Self-review of round seventy: B, identifiers and ranges

Working copy `(scratch)` at `76dcd55`. Trial fix with tests: `B-fix.diff` (six source files, four new test files, one test's wording updated; full unit suite 3,358 passed with it, `tsc` clean on the changed files). Data used: Kew's own `wcvp.zip` (4 June 2026, one download), GBIF's backbone `simple.txt.gz` (the August 2023 backbone the API serves, one download), `/api/index` once, 280 live dossiers, and about 120 single requests to GBIF, Wikidata and IPNI, paced. ## Findings

**1. Ixora coccinea still shows Embothrium coccineum's range, and the 70c family rule can never fire on Kew's files (confirmed).** `authors.ts:136` `inFamily` compares each own row's `family` with the backbone's. `bulk.ts:61` reads that field from Kew's `wcvp_names.csv`, and Kew files a synonym or invalid name under the family of its own genus, not of its accepted entry: row 103355, "Ixora coccinea Comm. ex Lam.", status Invalid, family **Rubiaceae**, accepted id 2788267 (Embothrium coccineum, Proteaceae). GBIF's copy of WCVP gives the same row as SYNONYM in Proteaceae, and the 70c test (`r70c-wikidata-ipni.test.ts:67`) was built from GBIF's copy, so it passes while the real files fail. Since a row of the species' own words always has the species' own genus, Kew always files it in the species' family: on Kew's files `inFamily` returns its input for every species. Measured: the bulk rule with the `inFamily` call removed gives the same entry for all 8,947 species. `kewIpni` reads GBIF's copy, where the family is the accepted entry's, which is why the IPNI came out right and the range did not. The comment at `bulk.ts:34` ("a synonym's, its accepted entry's") and the `/about/how` sentence on Ixora (`about/how/+page.svelte:36`) describe what the code does not do. The live page: native Argentina South, Chile Central, Chile South; Kew "shrub or tree, temperate"; 0 of 1,749 records in range. Kew's Ixora coccinea L. has 7 regions, "shrub, wet tropical". Reproduction: `zz-b70-kew-family.test.ts` with Kew's verbatim rows. Fix (in the diff): `inFamily` takes the family of the entry a row is filed under (`familyOf`), and `ownRows` passes the accepted entry's family. Over all 8,947 species this changes Ixora coccinea and nothing else.

**2. About 16 species sit on a GBIF key that is not the familiar plant's name, and four show it plainly (confirmed).** `scripts/bulk-fetch.ts:85` matches the bare name, and GBIF's match takes the accepted usage of the words. For Rubus parviflorus that is 7872219, "Rubus parviflorus Kük." (1938, published as a subspecies, "possible variant of R. parviflorus Hegetschw."), with no synonyms. Nuttall's name, the thimbleberry, is 2989557, a synonym GBIF files under Rubus nutkanus (2994229, 59 synonyms). The live page is a thimbleberry page with Kükenthal's author: range is Kew's R. parviflorus Nutt. (by the "one accepted entry" fallback); the 449 open records and 66 photographs are thimbleberry records (GBIF files the 54,951 records named without an author under 7872219); summary and iNaturalist 51646 are the thimbleberry's; but the name line reads "Kük.", there is no common name and no synonym, the GBIF link is Kükenthal's record, and Wikidata, POWO, IPNI and WFO are refused. Search for "thimbleberry" returns Rubus occidentalis and R. odoratus only. Over the reference, comparing each key's author with Kew's accepted author and with GBIF's other usages of the words (offline, all species): Ranunculus ficaria (Walter, 1788, 0 synonyms; L. is under Ficaria verna subsp. verna, 45; "lesser celandine" and "Ficaria verna" find nothing), Myrica cerifera (Bigelow; L. under Morella cerifera, 37; "wax myrtle" finds only Morella californica), Myrica rubra (A.Chev.), Hoodia gordonii (Sweet, 1830), Stapelia grandiflora (Curtis, 1802), Piaranthus punctatus (R.Br.), Stapelia gettliffei, Philodendron bipinnatifidum (Schott ex Kunth), Weingartia steinbachii (Werderm.; 104 synonyms on subsp. steinbachii), Fragaria virginiana (Duchesne, invalid), Ixora coccinea (the invalid Comm. ex Lam.; L. is DOUBTFUL 2905493), and four whose familiar author GBIF files under another species of this reference (Adenia gummifera, Drosera planchonii, Nepenthes gantungensis, Titanopsis primosii). Six more sit on a species key whose synonyms GBIF holds on the type subspecies (Claytonia perfoliata, Crassula alpestris, Cyphostemma junceum, Mammillaria pennispinosa, M. schiedeana, Syzygium cordatum). About 45 further "differences" are citation variants of one name and harmless. Fix: see the root cause of issue 3 below.

**3. Fragaria virginiana shows one subspecies' range (confirmed).** `bulk.ts:205` `accepted` takes the entry of the own row by author: Kew's "Fragaria virginiana Duchesne" (Invalid) is filed under F. virginiana subsp. virginiana, so the page shows 45 regions where Kew's species, F. virginiana Mill., has 66 (subsp. glauca, grayana and platypetala missing). Fix (in the diff): an own row filed under an infraspecific entry of these very words gives way to the one species entry Kew accepts by the words in the family. Over the reference it changes Fragaria virginiana only. Test in `zz-b70-kew-family.test.ts`.

**4. About 480 pages show the range of a Kew entry under another name and do not say so; five show another reference species' range (confirmed).** Comparing every live `origin` with Kew's accepted entry of the exact name in the family: 8,388 agree; 35 come from Kew's entry of the same name filed in another family (the same plant in Kew's broader Boraginaceae, Polypodiaceae, Aspleniaceae and the like); 482 come from a synonym or invalid row whose accepted entry is another name. Of these, 445 are homotypic (Agave brunnea as Manfreda brunnea: the same plant) and 37 heterotypic (Kew sinks GBIF's species into another: Ceropegia mixta as Orbea variegata, Prunus glandulifolia as P. maackii; four of them through a name Kew holds more than once, Ixora among them). Five show the range and Kew description of another species that has its own page here: Ceropegia melanops (C. nigra's 14 countries), Copiapoa boliviana (C. calderana), Drosera parvula (D. paleacea), Echeveria sessiliflora (E. pinetorum), Weingartia pulchra (W. canigueralii). The bulk fetcher returns the entry only on the older-name path (`bulk.ts:642`); the own-row path (`bulk.ts:623`) does not, so `build.ts:247` writes no detail and the source reads "WCVP (Govaerts, RBG Kew) via GBIF" (six pages read). Under rule 1 the page should say "Kew holds this name as a synonym of X; the range is X's". Smallest fix: return `entry: entryLabel(got.entry)` when the entry's name is not the species', and write it as the older-name path does.

**5. None of the 40 is another plant on the IPNI side except possibly Redouté's Lachenalia. Across all 594, the pages that link another author's name are the five where the IPNI is Kew's invalid or illegitimate homonym, still live: Colchicum neapolitanum (Orph. ex Nyman), Lachenalia pusilla (Eckl. ex Kunth), Ceropegia spiralis (Hook.f. & Thomson), Talinum lineare (Hoffmanns.), Mammillaria schiedeana (Pfeiff.); Origanum majorana and Drosera binata link Majorana hortensis and D. dichotoma; Mammillaria magnifica's POWO is Sencke's unplaced name. ## Root causes of the open issues

1. **Ixora coccinea.** Kew's `family` column on non-accepted rows is the genus's (13,801 of 969,871 such rows differ from their entry's family; a row of the species' own words shares its genus, and so its family). The 70c test copied GBIF's copy, where it is the entry's. Fix: finding 1. Rebuild 8793122.
2. **`--list-rebuild`.** Ixora was not listed because the rule did not change its answer; in general it lists rule changes, not stale pages. Fix: finding 8.
3. **Rubus parviflorus.** The names file is matched without authors, and GBIF's match picks the accepted usage, which for these words is Kükenthal's orphan. A reference-level remap means: matching the names file with Kew's author for the words; a key alias table (old key to new) read when the tracker resolves a grower's `taxonKey`, never rewriting the log; new slugs with redirects from the old names; a new occurrence download covering the new keys; rebuilds; and a choice the owner must make, since the remap follows GBIF's taxonomy and Rubus parviflorus would become Rubus nutkanus, Ranunculus ficaria Ficaria verna, Myrica cerifera Morella cerifera. Smaller and enough for the four visible cases: keep the keys, and where the key's author is plainly another than Kew's accepted entry for the words, show Kew's author and read synonyms and common names from GBIF's usage by Kew's author (2989557), saying so; add this test to `--list-rebuild`. My list in finding 2 is the starting set.
4. **The six refusals.** Wrong: finding 6.
5. **IPNI/POWO.** Mostly IPNI duplicates; the wrong links are those in finding 5.

## Checked and sound

- With the base code and Kew's files, the bulk range rule reproduces the live native range for all 8,917 species it answers (30 go by older names or are ambiguous): the corpus is consistent with the code.
- The 35 family-name differences between GBIF and Kew are untouched by `inFamily`, with or without my fix.
- `kewIpni` on GBIF's copy gives Ixora 753844-1, as live.
- Five inactive-id follows in the 240-dossier sample (Marginatocereus, Sclerocactus intertextus, Pouteria campechiana, Albuca toxicaria, Avonia ruschii) are each right; the 70c slip cases still pass under the stricter rule.
- IPNI pacing (1.1 s) is set, and IPNI answers the builder's user agent (it refuses Python's default).
- Dated lag keeps work live (Harpephyllum caffrum keeps its iNaturalist id, naming 13 October).
