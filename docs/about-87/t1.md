## /about/formats (section "The fields, by kind", h3 `#f-accession`, after `olderKindOf`)

- `item` and `itemOf`: an offset, division or bulb split of an accession, filed as a plant of its own. Its number is the accession's and a letter, "2026-0012/A". `item` is the letter, A to ZZ, and `itemOf` the record id of the plant that holds the bare number.
- The plants of one accession share their name, identification and provenance: an edit of any of these on one of them is written to each in the same commit. Each keeps its own space, status, notes, log and photographs.
- `deacc` (died, given, lost, failed, stolen, reaccessioned): why the plant is no longer held; failed is a propagation that failed. `deaccOn` is the day, YYYY-MM-DD, YYYY-MM or YYYY, and `deaccNote` a note of up to 1,000 characters. The status is set with it: dead for died and failed, archived otherwise.
- `provType` (W, Z, G, U) and `propHistory` (I, S, SA, SB, SC, V, VA, VB, U) are fields E.1 and E.2 of ITF2, the International Transfer Format for Botanic Garden Plant Records, version 2 (BGCI and TDWG). Provenance Type and Propagation History, in ITF2's codes.
- `collector`: the collector's name (ITF2 F.24). `collected`: the collection date (F.27), YYYY-MM-DD, YYYY-MM or YYYY. The collector's own number is `fieldNumber`, kept as typed.
- `idLevel` (U, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4): ITF2's Verification Level (D.1). `idBy`: who identified the plant (D.2). `idOn`: when (D.3), a day, a month or a year.
- `exChain`: an array of the plant's earlier holders, the most recent first, at most 12, each up to 80 characters. `clone`: a clone's name, apart from a cultivar.
- `idHistory`: an array of the names the plant had before, the oldest first, at most 30. Each entry is an object with "name", "until" (the day it stopped being the plant's name), and the "idLevel", "idBy" and "idOn" it had then.
- Each of these is read and checked when it is read. A value of another type, a word or code this version does not know, or a day that is not one, is shown as not stated and kept in the log as it is.

## /about/how

### "Your collection" (h2 `#collection`, the rules in full, before "The formats page has the details")

- (`#items`) An offset, division or bulb split can be split off a plant as an item of its accession: a plant of its own under the accession's number and the next free letter, 2026-0012/A, then /B. A letter is never given twice.
- The plants of one accession share their name, identification and provenance, so a change to one is saved on each. Each keeps its own space, log and photographs.
- (`#no-longer-held`) A plant you no longer have is recorded as no longer held, with a reason (died, given away, lost, propagation failed, stolen, reaccessioned), a day and a note. Nothing is deleted: its page stays, with Restore, and it is listed under No longer held.
- Recording a death records the plant as no longer held, died, on the death's day. Remove is for a record made by mistake, and a removed plant is listed under No longer held too, with Restore.
- (`#itf2`) Provenance can be given in the codes of ITF2, the International Transfer Format botanic gardens use: the provenance type (W, Z, G or U) and the propagation history. Where your own provenance word means the same in ITF2, the plant's page shows that code and says it was read from the word.
- Wild-collected and F1 are read as W; cultivated seed and vegetative, which ITF2 splits into Z and G, are not read as a type. A pair of codes ITF2 does not allow together, or a code your provenance word contradicts, is refused with the reason.
- (`#field-numbers`) A field number is kept as typed, and read as the collector's code and number where it reads as one: SB 829 is SB and 829, KK1398 is KK and 1398.
- A change of name keeps the name the plant had, the day it stopped and how it had been identified, under Earlier names. A qualifier in the name (cf., aff., nr.) is shown with what it means; sp. names the genus only.
- The plants sheet carries all of these, under ITF2's column names where ITF2 has a field (prot, prohis, cnam, cid, cdat, idql, vlev, vnam, vdat, vhist), and the import reads them back.

### "What the site knows about you" (h2 `#privacy`, after the "Is it an older name?" item)

- (`#bcss`) A plant page's "Look up at BCSS Field Number Database" sends that plant's field number to the British Cactus and Succulent Society's database only when you press it. It opens in a new tab, without this page's address. The page never asks BCSS anything by itself.
